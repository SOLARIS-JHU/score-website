01 / The bottleneck

A simple condition.
A hard global search.

A Lyapunov sublevel set Ωρ is safe when its derivative remains negative. Because checking every point in the volume is redundant, certification reduces to locating the largest derivative on the boundary manifold ℳ.

$$\gamma^*=\max_{x\in\mathcal{M}}\dot V(x)$$

If γ* < 0, the region is certified.

That maximum is non-convex and expensive to find. Grid search, SMT, and sum-of-squares methods offer deterministic guarantees, but encounter severe scaling limits in dense, high-dimensional systems.

03 / Why Weibull?

Compact geometry gives the tail an endpoint.

Under stationary sampling and regular local geometry near a nondegenerate maximum, the optimality gap behaves locally like a scaled chi-squared random variable. That places the upper tail of V̇ in the Weibull maximum domain of attraction.

Theorem 1
$$1-F_Y(y)\asymp (y_{\max}-y)^{d/2}$$

As \(y \to y_{\max}^{-}\), the tail decays toward a finite right endpoint.

Certificate conditions

  • 01
    CoverageChains must reach the basin containing the global maximum.
  • 02
    ConvergenceBlocks must be large enough to reach the GEV limit.
  • 03
    CompactnessThe level-set boundary must be closed and bounded.
  • 04
    Fit validationThe empirical maxima must pass the KS goodness-of-fit test.
What the result means

SCORE provides a conditional statistical certificate—not an unconditional deterministic proof. Its confidence statement depends on the sampling, geometry, convergence, and inference assumptions above.

05 / Abstract

The paper
in brief.

Certifying the Region of Attraction for high-dimensional nonlinear dynamical systems remains a severe computational bottleneck. Traditional deterministic methods provide hard guarantees but typically fail to scale beyond 20 dimensions.

SCORE integrates Projected Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics with Extreme Value Theory, framing certification as constrained extreme-value estimation. Under stationary sampling and regular local geometry, the Lyapunov derivative belongs to the Weibull maximum domain of attraction, enabling a finite-endpoint estimate and an upper confidence bound.

Experiments show certification tightness competitive with exact Sum of Squares programming on a 2D benchmark and successful application to a dense 500-dimensional ODE at a nominal 99.99% confidence level.
